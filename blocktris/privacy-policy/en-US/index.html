This privacy policy explains how Stack Rush handles information for the Android app published under package name com.makeitbetter.stackrush.

Effective date: 2026-10-24

Leaderboard data deletion · Support and publisher information

Optional-feature operating notice: the 22 October 2026 review prepares Android rewarded ads for use within the reviewed child-protected, non-personalized scope and the player's applicable choices. Availability depends on the installed version, Google request eligibility and ad inventory. New leaderboard processing and Apps in Toss advertising remain on hold for separate operational checks. 1. What the app does

Stack Rush is an all-ages single-player arcade game. It is playable without creating an account, signing in, or completing an age check. The app does not ask for, determine, or store a player's date of birth or age profile. Every player receives 10 plays per UTC day. After those plays are used, any player may voluntarily watch an optional rewarded ad to recharge 10 plays where the feature is available. The online daily leaderboard is a separate feature; new participation, retrieval and uploads remain held pending its operating review. Its existing-data and reactivation behavior below requires the player's separate choice.

2. Data stored on your device

The app stores a small amount of gameplay data locally on the device so the game can remember values such as your remaining plays, last recorded height, best recorded height, cumulative total height, and the UTC day used for the daily reset. The app does not store a date of birth, age, or age-profile result. If a player separately joins the daily leaderboard, the app stores the leaderboard policy version, whether a decision was made, the opt-in choice, the decision time, and a server-issued leaderboard identity consisting of a private player secret, player ID, and fixed game alias. A stable leaderboard player ID or secret is not created before opt-in. Local ad preferences record the choice version, decision and change time separately from Google consent. A legacy withdrawal-cleanup marker remains for earlier deletion requests; an old acceptance value is ignored by basic gameplay.

Android backup and device-transfer backup are disabled for the app, so this local save data stays in the app's storage on that device unless you remove the app, clear its storage through Android settings, or the operating system removes the app data.

3. Online leaderboard

New leaderboard processing remains held in current review-aware builds. The following describes existing data and behavior when that separate feature can resume. Existing authenticated deletion and rights requests remain available.

For players who separately choose to join, Stack Rush can submit daily leaderboard records to a hosted leaderboard endpoint. The score submission includes the server-issued player ID, UTC day key, total height, best run height, max combo, score, and app version. The service uses its own UTC day, submission time, and fixed alias rather than trusting those values from the device.

The service generates an alias in the fixed Rook-XXXXXXXX format; it is not a free-text profile field and cannot be changed by a score submission. Public leaderboard responses show ranked aliases and scores but do not expose the private authentication player ID. Choosing to use rewarded advertising does not also join the leaderboard. The app does not create a leaderboard identity or submit or retrieve leaderboard data before the player explicitly opts in.

The leaderboard issues a signed guest-session token containing the player ID, issuance and expiration times, and a random nonce. It expires after 10 minutes, is cached only in app-process memory, and is not written to device storage by the app or stored as a session row by the service.

The app sends the private player secret only to obtain a session or request deletion. The service does not store that raw secret. It stores a signing-secret HMAC credential hash, the internal player ID, the fixed alias, creation time, and last session time.

To prevent abuse, the leaderboard converts the request IP address into a secret-salted SHA-256 rate-limit key. The leaderboard application does not store the raw IP address in its application database. Cloudflare may separately process request metadata in its service and security logs. Rate-limit rows expire after one hour and are removed during later requests and by an hourly cleanup job, so deletion can occur up to the next cleanup run after expiration.

Daily score records are retained for up to 30 UTC days. A leaderboard credential that has not obtained a session for 180 days is deleted together with its remaining scores. The hourly cleanup job performs these removals, so deletion can occur on the next cleanup run after a retention threshold is reached.

On 24 October 2026, we confirmed that the deployed hourly cleanup job's last 10 runs succeeded. Aggregate counts were zero for scores, credentials and rate-limit records past their cleanup deadline, and for scores without a corresponding credential. The current Workers Free plan provides a seven-day D1 Time Travel recovery window. This Worker's Logs, Traces and Issues were off, with no separate export destinations configured. These are settings and counts observed at that time, not proof that provider security logs or backup history have been erased.

The procedure for reapplying erasure requests after recovery has not yet been verified. We do not restore historical backups into service unless reapplication of erasure requests and retention cleanup can be verified. New leaderboard processing remains on hold while this recovery condition and the leaderboard's child-processing and international-transfer assessment remain unresolved.

4. Advertising

After the 10 daily plays are used, any player can voluntarily choose to use Google AdMob rewarded advertising to recharge 10 plays. The recharge is granted only after the rewarded-ad completion callback. Choosing not to watch an ad does not join the leaderboard or create an advertising account; the regular 10-play allowance returns at the next UTC daily reset.

Before the ads SDK is initialized or an ad is requested, Stack Rush applies one child-safe configuration to every player: ads are requested as non-personalized and child-directed, the request is tagged as under the age of consent, and the maximum ad content rating is G. When you first request an optional rewarded ad, the app contacts Google to update the advertising privacy choices that apply, and waits for completion of any required form before an eligible request. You can reopen required privacy choices through Ad settings under the persistent Settings entry. The automatic AdMob startup provider is removed from the Android build so these settings can be applied before initialization.

Google and its advertising partners may process approximate location derived from IP address, app interactions, ad interactions, diagnostics, and device or other identifiers for ad delivery, measurement, security, and fraud prevention under their policies. The Android advertising ID permission (com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID) is removed from the app manifest, so Stack Rush does not request access to the Android Advertising ID. Removing that permission does not prevent Google from processing the other limited data described above.

The app does not send a name, email address, date of birth, GPS position or leaderboard ID to Google for advertising. Google's child treatment restricts personalized and remarketing ads, third-party ad-vendor requests and advertising-ID transmission. This reviewed scope does not include unreviewed mediation or separate ad SDKs and provides no adult personalized-ad route.

Google determines retention by data type, purpose and legal or security needs. Its general retention policy gives examples of removing part of IP addresses from advertising server logs after nine months and cookie information after eighteen months; encrypted deletion backups may retain data for up to six months. These are not a single deletion deadline for every AdMob record or an app-controlled immediate-erasure promise. See Google's retention and deletion policy.

5. Data the app does not intentionally collect

Based on the current app implementation, Stack Rush does not intentionally collect or transmit account information such as your name, email address, phone number, precise location, contacts, photos, microphone input, or in-app account credentials. The app does not include in-game account creation, chat, in-app purchases, or precise location sharing.

6. Permissions, network access, and platform services

The Android app manifest declares INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, and WAKE_LOCK for the game engine and the optional online features. The Android advertising ID permission and the Android Privacy Sandbox ad ID, attribution, and topics permissions are explicitly removed from the merged app manifest. Stack Rush therefore does not request access to those advertising identifiers or interest-topic APIs.

The app manifest explicitly removes legacy shared-storage, phone-state, boot-completed, and foreground-service permissions contributed by transitive libraries. Stack Rush does not use those capabilities.

Based on the current implementation, the game code does not ask for camera, microphone, contacts, photo library, or precise location access, and it does not implement user accounts, chat, or in-app purchases.

If you download the app through Google Play, Google may process technical or diagnostic information under its own policies when handling downloads, updates, app signing, crash reporting, device compatibility, or store operations. Those platform-level processes are outside the direct control of Stack Rush.

7. Children

Stack Rush uses the same child-safe treatment for every player rather than asking for age. The app does not request, store, or transmit a date of birth or age profile. Every player receives the same 10-play daily allowance and may voluntarily choose the optional rewarded-ad recharge described above. All ad requests use the non-personalized, child-directed, under-age-of-consent, maximum-G configuration. Leaderboard participation remains a separate explicit choice, and no leaderboard identity is created before that choice.

The all-ages content rating and child-safe ad flags do not establish verified parental permission. The app has no verified guardian-consent flow. For consent-based processing of children under 14 in Korea, a legal representative's consent and verification are required. Android advertising is assessed within the minimum scope of delivering a directly requested ad and preventing abuse, with child interests and safeguards considered separately. It does not include behavioral profiling or marketing contact. Required consent for non-essential device access and applicable child protections are not waived by an age-treatment flag. New leaderboard processing remains separately held.

9. Data deletion requests

To request deletion of Stack Rush online leaderboard data, email support@mibstudio.top and include the app name and your Rook-XXXXXXXX leaderboard alias. The publisher can use the protected administrator deletion route to delete the matching credential and all of its leaderboard records without asking you to disclose the private player ID or secret.

In review-aware builds, use ID · Manage on the game-result screen to leave. Uploads stop first. A deletion request is sent using the existing credential. Until an HTTP 200 response confirms deletion, the minimal credential is retained for retry on the next launch or management action, and re-enrollment stays blocked. The app shows Retry deletion while pending. Provider logs and backups are separate. Older builds may erase the local credential even when a server request fails; clearing storage or uninstalling is not server deletion.

Your public alias alone is not proof of ownership. The publisher may require minimal additional verification and will communicate completion or a retention exception. Do not send your private player secret. You or a guardian may also request access, correction, restriction or withdrawal through the same contact.

Local gameplay data stored on your device can be deleted by uninstalling the app or clearing the app's storage in Android settings. Advertising data handled by Google AdMob or Google Play is controlled through Google's privacy and advertising controls.

9. Ad settings and privacy choices

Basic play starts without blanket privacy acceptance. The persistent Settings entry provides controls for: Sound, Language, Ad settings, Privacy policy and Close. Ad settings let you turn local rewarded-ad use off or on and reopen any required Google privacy choices, including while local ads are off. Changing, declining or cancelling a choice does not close the game or require blanket acceptance on the next launch.

Where ads are available, the first ad action explains IP addresses, ad interactions, diagnostics and overseas processing. The local feature setting is not Google consent or a TCF receipt. Each app session waits for a current UMP update and any required form completion on an explicit ad request. canRequestAds() is request eligibility, not personalized-ad consent. The Google SDK uses CMP choice signals to select eligible non-personalized or limited ads. The current app retains CHILD/G/under-age protection and provides no adult personalized-ad path.

Non-personalized ads can still involve data processing for frequency limits and measurement. Limited ads can still use IP and delivery data and, depending on the account's programmatic limited ads setting, invalid-traffic-only storage. Neither mode means no data processing or guaranteed ad availability. Ineligible or unfilled ads grant no plays; 10 extra plays are granted only after a confirmed earned-reward callback. Basic daily plays and leaderboard choices remain independent. See Google's non-personalized ads and limited ads guidance.

Leaderboard withdrawal and deletion remain in the leaderboard screen. Previously requested deletion retains minimal credentials for retries and is not cancelled by a new ad choice or enrollment attempt. Turning ads off stops new local requests; it is not erasure of all data already held by Google. Local game records stay on the device. Changing ad settings does not lift the separate leaderboard or Apps in Toss holds.
